ACORN WOODPECKER FACTS

Description:
The Acorn Woodpecker is a striking woodpecker with a black and white face, bright red crown, and olive-green back. Males have a red crown extending to the nape, while females show a yellow-and-black patch. Both sexes display prominent white eye-rings, black chin, and white rump patches. Size: 8-9 inches long.

Behavior:
Acorn Woodpeckers are highly social and live in cooperative family groups. They are known for drilling thousands of small holes in trees to store acorns, creating distinctive "granaries." They communicate with loud, raucous calls and engage in acrobatic foraging displays.

Habitat:
Found primarily in oak woodlands and mixed forests from Oregon to Mexico. They prefer areas with large oak trees that provide acorns for food storage. Prefer elevations between 2,000-8,000 feet in diverse woodland ecosystems.

Similar Looking Birds:
Can be confused with other woodpeckers like Pileated or Hairy Woodpeckers, but the distinctive red crown, white face pattern, and olive-green back are unique to Acorn Woodpeckers. Their social behavior and visible granaries help distinguish them.

Best Ways to Identify:
Look for the striking white face with red crown, olive back, and white rump. Listen for loud, nasal calls. Search for distinctive granaries—trees covered with small holes. Their social behavior and noisy groups make them conspicuous in oak woodlands.
