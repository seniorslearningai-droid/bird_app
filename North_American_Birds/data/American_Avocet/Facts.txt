American Avocet (Recurvirostra americana)
==========================================

Description
-----------
Elegant wading bird with distinctive upturned bill and striking plumage pattern. Black and white back and wings, white underparts, and rusty-chestnut head and neck during breeding season. Slender, long legs are blue-gray. Medium-sized and streamlined.

Behavior
--------
Highly social and gregarious, often seen in flocks both in breeding and non-breeding seasons. Feeds by sweeping its curved bill side-to-side through shallow water. Swift and agile flier, often flying in formation. Males perform elaborate courtship displays.

Habitat
-------
Prefers shallow alkaline lakes, salt marshes, and coastal lagoons. Breeds in loose colonies on sandy or rocky islands. Found in western North America, particularly in prairie regions and Great Basin. Migratory, wintering in southern coastal areas.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
American Avocet might be confused with other wading birds like stilts or whimbrels. However, its distinctive upturned bill and black-and-white pattern make it quite recognizable. Other avocet species occur in different geographic regions entirely.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Look for the unique upturned bill, distinctive black-and-white plumage on back and wings, and long slender legs. The rusty head and neck appear in breeding plumage. Feeding behavior of sweeping bill side-to-side is very characteristic and distinctive among North American birds.
