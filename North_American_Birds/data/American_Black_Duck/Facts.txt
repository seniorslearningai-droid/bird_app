American Black Duck (Anas rubripes)
===================================

Description
-----------
A dark dabbling duck with predominantly blackish-brown plumage on head and body.
Males have bright reddish-purple bill and pale yellow feet. Females are similar but
slightly duller. In flight, pale underwings contrast sharply with dark body.

Behavior
--------
Dabbling duck that feeds by tipping forward to reach submerged vegetation. Highly
social, often found in pairs or flocks. Dabbles in shallow water and grazes on land.
Males perform elaborate courtship displays. Relatively vocal with quacking calls.

Habitat
-------
Inhabits freshwater marshes, swamps, ponds, and rivers in eastern North America.
Prefers shallow water with aquatic vegetation. Winters in coastal areas and open water.
Breeds in northeastern wetlands from Nova Scotia to Maine and adjacent regions.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Male Mallards have green head and white chest, unlike black duck's dark plumage. Female
Mallards show more contrast in pattern. Scoters are larger with different silhouette.
Pintails have elongated necks. Wigeon species lack dark coloring entirely.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Look for very dark overall plumage with pale underwings visible in flight. Males show
bright red-purple bill and yellow feet. White-spotted cheeks distinguish from Mallards.
Dabbling behavior at shallow edges. Pale eye-ring and head shape are also distinctive.
