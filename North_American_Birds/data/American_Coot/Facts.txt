American Coot (Fulica americana)
=================================

Description
-----------
Medium-sized dark gray waterbird with black head and neck.
White bill and forehead shield. Distinctive red eye with
white inner ring. Compact body adapted for swimming.

Behavior
--------
Highly social and gregarious, often in large flocks.
Excellent swimmers but clumsy in flight. Makes loud
honking and cackling calls. Feeds by dabbling and diving
in shallow water and on land.

Habitat
-------
Found on freshwater lakes, ponds, marshes, and wetlands.
Prefers open water with emergent vegetation. Ranges from
southern Canada to Central America. Common in park ponds
and urban water bodies.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Could be confused with Common Gallinule (red shield).
Gallinules have red bills and shields, Coots are white.
Juvenile Coots are lighter brown. Swimming waterfowl like
grebes have different body shapes and neck lengths.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Look for distinctive white bill and frontal shield on
black head. Bright red eye is key field mark. Note white
under-tail coverts visible when swimming. Listen for
loud cackling calls year-round.
