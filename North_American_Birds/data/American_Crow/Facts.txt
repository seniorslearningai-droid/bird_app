American Crow (Corvus brachyrhynchos)
====================================

Description
-----------
The American Crow is a large, all-black bird with a distinctive squared-off tail.
Its plumage is uniformly black with a slight iridescent sheen. Crows have a stout,
thick black bill and dark brown to black eyes. They measure about 16-21 inches long.

Behavior
--------
American Crows are highly intelligent and social birds, often found in flocks called
"murders." They are opportunistic foragers, eating insects, seeds, nuts, fruit, and
carrion. Known for problem-solving abilities and complex vocalizations beyond simple
caws. They can remember human faces and hold "grudges."

Habitat
-------
Crows thrive in diverse environments including forests, woodlands, farmland, parks,
and urban areas. They are highly adaptable and found throughout North America from
southern Canada to northern Mexico. They prefer open areas with nearby trees for
nesting and roosting.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
American Crows are often confused with ravens, which are larger with wedge-shaped tails.
Fish Crows are smaller and have a different call. Jays are colorful with crests. Grackles
are smaller with longer tails. Black Phoebes are much smaller. Starlings are smaller
with pointed bills.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Listen for the distinctive loud "caw-caw-caw" call. Look for the squared-off tail
in flight, unlike ravens' wedge shape. Observe size compared to other birds—larger
than most songbirds. Watch for their habit of walking on ground. Crows often
gather in large flocks, especially at dusk.
