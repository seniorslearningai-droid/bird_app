American Dipper (Cinclus mexicanus)
====================================

Description
-----------
Small, stocky songbird with dark slate-gray plumage overall.
Distinctive rounded body shape with short tail and neck.
White eyelids flash when blinking. Bob their bodies constantly.

Behavior
--------
Unique habit of walking underwater on stream bottoms to find food.
Dive and submerge in fast-moving water while hunting for insects.
Sing beautiful melodious songs, often near flowing water habitats.
Remain active year-round instead of migrating seasonally elsewhere.

Habitat
-------
Fast-moving mountain streams and rivers with clear cool water.
Prefer rocky areas with waterfalls, cascades, and turbulent flow.
Found from sea level to high alpine elevations in western ranges.
Require streams with good water quality and adequate food sources.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Wrentit smaller with longer tail and different habitat preference.
Bushtit similarly small but found in dry chaparral, not streams.
Water-thrush breeds in eastern forests, different appearance.
Ouzel is another name for this species, not similar to anything.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Look for stocky dark bird bobbing while standing on rocks.
Watch for birds diving and walking on stream bottoms underwater.
Listen for loud melodious songs near fast-moving mountain water.
Note white eyelid flashes when bird blinks its eyes distinctly.
