American Kestrel (Falco sparverius)
===================================

Description
-----------
Tiny falcon with rufous-brown back and blue-gray wings. Males have blue wing coverts;
females lack this feature. Both have distinctive facial marks resembling a mustache.
Underparts appear buff to white with sparse markings.

Behavior
--------
Hovers above open fields hunting small prey. Hunts from perches and catches insects,
small lizards, and birds mid-flight. Often seen perched on telephone wires and posts.
Males perform dramatic diving displays during courtship season to attract females.

Habitat
-------
Open woodlands, grasslands, agricultural areas, and deserts. Favors open landscapes
with scattered perches. Found in meadows, prairies, and cultivated fields across
North and South America year-round or seasonally.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Merlin is larger and lacks rufous coloring. Sharp-shinned Hawk is bulkier with longer
tail. Eurasian Kestrel males have solid gray heads. Female American Kestrels lack the
blue-gray wing pattern that males display prominently.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Look for tiny falcon size with distinctive facial marks. Hovering flight is characteristic
of this species. Males show bright blue wing coverts in flight. Listen for rapid
"klee-klee-klee" call. Rufous back contrasts with blue-gray wings on males.
