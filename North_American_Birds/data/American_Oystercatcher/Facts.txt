American Oystercatcher (Haematopus palliatus)
============================================

Description
-----------
Large shorebird with striking black and white plumage. Head and upper body
are black, contrasting sharply with white belly and wing patches. Long,
straight, bright orange-red bill and legs. Eyes have golden-yellow iris.

Behavior
--------
Highly vocal and defensive of nesting territories. Forages along beaches
and mudflats probing or hammering at shells to extract mollusks. Often
seen in small flocks outside breeding season. Mates for life, often staying
with same partner for many years.

Habitat
-------
Found along coastal areas, beaches, sandy shores, and rocky coastlines.
Prefers salt marshes, tidal flats, and open beaches for feeding and nesting.
Ranges from Atlantic and Gulf coasts to Pacific waters throughout North and
South America.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Black Oystercatcher (Pacific coast) differs in all-black plumage without
white belly. Other shorebirds like plovers and sandpipers are much smaller.
The distinctive orange bill sets them apart from almost all other species.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Look for large black and white shorebird with unmistakable long orange-red
bill and orange legs. Listen for loud, sharp "kleep-kleep-kleep" calls.
Often seen in pairs or small groups probing beaches and mudflats for food.
