American Pipit (Anthus rubescens)
================================

Description
-----------
Small songbird with streaked, buffy-brown plumage and fine, pointed bill.
Pale underparts with streaking on breast and flanks. Darker back with
buff coloring. Slender build adapted for ground walking.

Behavior
--------
Walks on ground with bobbing tail, rarely perches in trees. Often sings
from high perches during breeding season. Forages by walking and picking
insects from ground. Highly migratory, traveling between breeding and
wintering grounds.

Habitat
-------
Breeds in alpine and arctic tundra above treeline. Prefers open, barren
terrain with sparse vegetation. Uses steep slopes, ridges, and rocky areas.
Winters in grasslands, desert scrub, and agricultural areas at lower elevations.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Resembles Sprague's Pipit but American Pipit has a slightly heavier bill
and different call. Red-throated Pipit in Asia. Lacks yellow in wings
unlike Meadow Pipit. Similar streaking pattern to some warbler species.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Listen for distinctive high-pitched calls and flight songs. Observe bobbing
tail motion when walking. Look for outer tail feathers with white spots.
Note thin, pointed bill. Watch for habit of walking on ground rather than
perching in vegetation. Breeding season males sing from exposed perches.
