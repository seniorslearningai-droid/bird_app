American Redstart (Setophaga ruticilla)
========================================

Description
-----------
Small warbler with striking plumage. Males display black
head and back with bright orange-red patches on wings, tail,
and flanks. Females are gray with yellow-orange markings
instead of red, making identification challenging for beginners.

Behavior
--------
Highly active flycatcher, constantly flitting and fanning tail
to flush insects. Males perform territorial songs from high perches
during breeding season. Females build cup-shaped nests in trees.
Often hover while catching prey mid-flight.

Habitat
-------
Deciduous and mixed forests during breeding in North America.
Prefers young forest growth and forest edges with dense
understory. Winters in Central and South American tropical forests.
Found from Canada through Panama seasonally.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Female Redstarts resemble female Black-throated Blue Warblers
and Northern Parula. Young males can confuse with female plumage.
Bay-breasted Warbler shares some coloring. Key differences are
in head pattern and tail shape.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Look for orange-red tail patches and constant tail-fanning behavior.
Males unmistakable with black and red coloring. Females show orange-yellow
patches on wings and tail. Active feeding style in mid-canopy
distinguishes from perching warblers.
