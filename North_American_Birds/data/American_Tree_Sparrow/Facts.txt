American Tree Sparrow (Spizelloides arborea)
=============================================

Description
-----------
Small songbird with reddish-brown cap, gray face, and prominent black spot on
white breast. Grayish-brown upperparts and pale underparts. Fine bill adapted
for seed-eating. Similar size to House Sparrow but more delicate appearance.

Behavior
--------
Feeds on seeds and small insects, often in flocks during migration and winter.
Males sing from exposed perches in spring. Forages on ground or low vegetation.
Highly vocal with clear, tinkling song. Social and may visit feeders in winter.

Habitat
-------
Breeds in Arctic and subarctic regions of northern Canada, Alaska. Winters across
central and eastern United States in open fields, prairies, marshes, and weedy areas.
Prefers brushy terrain and shrubland with scattered trees near feeding areas.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Chipping Sparrow has similar size and coloring but lacks black breast spot. Field
Sparrow has pink bill and reddish face. Rufous-crowned Sparrow larger with different
head pattern. All smaller than Fox or Song Sparrows with more delicate features.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Black spot on white breast is the key field mark. Reddish crown combined with gray
face distinctive. Listen for clear, tinkling song in spring. Check for flocking
behavior in winter. Size and delicate appearance help distinguish from similar species.
