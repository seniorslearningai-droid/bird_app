American White Pelican (Pelecanus erythrorhynchos)
===================================================

Description
-----------
Large white waterbird with black wing tips, long bill, and large throat pouch.
Breeding birds display bright orange-yellow bill and facial skin patches. White
plumage contrasts with black flight feathers. Measures up to 5.5 feet in length
with a wingspan exceeding 9 feet, making them among North America's largest birds.

Behavior
--------
Highly social birds, often found in flocks, hunting cooperatively by diving and
catching fish with their expansive throat pouch rather than spearing prey. They
are strong, graceful fliers that often soar on thermals at great heights. Pelicans
raft together on water and nest colonially in large groups on islands and sandbars.

Habitat
-------
Inhabit shallow freshwater lakes, reservoirs, and coastal waters. Breed on
remote islands and sandy/rocky shorelines across western and central North America.
Winter primarily on southern U.S. coasts and Mexico. Prefer open water bodies with
adequate fish populations and minimal human disturbance for nesting colonies.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Brown Pelicans share similar body shape but are smaller, darker, and dive-fish
individually from flight. Trumpeter Swans are all-white but lack the prominent
throat pouch and have longer necks. Great White Herons are slimmer with thinner
bills. Wood Storks have curved bills and fly differently.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Massive size and white plumage with black wing tips are diagnostic in flight.
Distinctive large throat pouch, visible even at distance, is unmistakable. The
massive bill, much longer than any other North American waterbird, is a key field
mark. Watch for characteristic low, level flight pattern and cooperative feeding
behavior in large flocks on open water.
