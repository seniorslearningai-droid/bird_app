American Wigeon (Mareca americana)
===================================

Description
-----------
A medium-sized dabbling duck with males displaying a distinctive glossy
green head and white crown, with creamy-buff body and pale blue-gray
bill. Females are grayish-brown with mottled plumage and lack the
striking head pattern of males.

Behavior
--------
American Wigeons are highly social ducks, often seen in flocks grazing
on land and water. They feed by dabbling and upending, sometimes grazing
like geese. Known for their distinctive high-pitched whistle, males
produce a three-note "whee-whew-whew" call during breeding season.

Habitat
-------
Prefers shallow freshwater marshes, wetlands, and prairie lakes during
breeding season. Winters in coastal bays, estuaries, river valleys, and
agricultural areas. They inhabit both interior wetlands and coastal
regions throughout North America.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Male American Wigeons can be confused with other dabbling ducks,
particularly Eurasian Wigeons which are rarer visitors. Females may
resemble female Mallards or Pintails but lack their distinctive patterns
and markings. Males are highly distinctive and unmistakable.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Males are unmistakable with the white-crowned head and glossy green
face. Listen for the characteristic whistle. Females can be identified
by the dark eye, pale gray body, and smaller, more delicate build
compared to similar dabbling duck species.
