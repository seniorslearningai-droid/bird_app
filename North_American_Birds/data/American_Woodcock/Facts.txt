American Woodcock (Scolopax minor)
===================================

Description
-----------
Small, stocky shorebird with a long slender bill. Mottled brown and buff
plumage provides excellent camouflage in leaf litter. Eyes positioned high
on head for upward vision while feeding.

Behavior
--------
Crepuscular and nocturnal forager. Males perform spectacular spring displays
with twisting flights and nasal vocalizations. Probes soil for earthworms
and invertebrates with distinctive up-and-down head bobbing motion.

Habitat
-------
Prefers young forests, aspen thickets, and brushy areas with moist soil.
Found in eastern North America from Atlantic coast to Great Plains. Requires
both open areas for displaying and dense cover for nesting and resting.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Wilson's Snipe has longer neck, different habitat preference. Both are
crepuscular shorebirds, but woodcock is more secretive. Common Snipe shows
more contrasting plumage patterns and prefers open wetland marshes.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Listen for distinctive nasal "peent" call at dusk during spring. Look for
characteristic round, stocky body shape. Long bill and high eye placement
are diagnostic. Flight displays involve rapid twittering wing sounds.
