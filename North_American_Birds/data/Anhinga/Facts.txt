Anhinga (Anhinga anhinga)
===========================

Description
-----------
The Anhinga is a long-necked waterbird with dark, glossy plumage. Males are
black with silvery-white markings on wings and back. Females have brown necks
and breasts contrasting with darker bodies. Straight, pointed yellow bills and
serpentine necks are distinctive.

Behavior
--------
Anhingas dive deep underwater to hunt fish, using their sharp bills like
spears. They swim with bodies submerged, showing only their heads and necks
above water. They are excellent swimmers but rarely fly, spending most time
perched on branches drying their wings.

Habitat
-------
Found in freshwater lakes, swamps, marshes, and coastal waterways across
the southeastern United States through Central and South America. They prefer
shallow water with trees and vegetation for nesting and roosting. Common in
cypress swamps and mangrove areas.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Cormorants share dark plumage and diving behavior, but have hooked bills and
thicker necks. Loons also dive for fish but are more compact. The Anhinga's
distinctive serpentine neck and straight spear-like bill set it apart from
these similar species.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Look for the characteristic long, thin neck held in an S-curve. The pointed,
straight yellow bill is diagnostic. Watch for their habit of swimming low in
water with only neck and head visible. Silvery wing patterns visible in
flight help distinguish males from cormorants.
