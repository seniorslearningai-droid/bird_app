Baltimore Oriole (Icterus galbula)
====================================

Description
-----------
Striking male is brilliant orange with black hood, back, wings, and tail;
white wing bars. Female is olive-yellow with two white wing bars.

Behavior
--------
Weaves a hanging pouch nest high in deciduous trees; eats insects, nectar,
and fruit; visits orange halves and grape jelly at feeders.

Habitat
-------
Open deciduous woodlands, forest edges, parks, and suburban areas of eastern
North America; winters in Central America and northern South America.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Orchard Oriole male is chestnut and black, not orange; Bullock's Oriole male
has large white wing patch and orange face; Hooded Oriole is slimmer.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Brilliant orange and black male; hanging woven nest; rich whistled song;
common along forest edges and suburbs in summer in the East.
