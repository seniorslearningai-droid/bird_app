Black-capped Vireo (Vireo atricapilla)
=======================================

Description
-----------
Small vireo with male showing glossy black cap, white spectacles, olive-green
back, and yellow-white underparts with yellow sides. Females have slate gray
cap. Red eye. About 4.5 inches. Endangered species.

Behavior
--------
Forages actively in dense shrubs, gleaning insects from leaves. Secretive
and hard to observe. Males sing persistently from dense cover in spring.

Habitat
-------
Scrubby oak and juniper thickets on rocky hillsides and canyons in Oklahoma,
Texas, and Coahuila, Mexico. Requires shrubs with patchy openings. Threatened
by Brown-headed Cowbird parasitism.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Blue-headed Vireo has blue-gray head without black cap. Ruby-crowned Kinglet
is smaller. No other vireo has solid black cap with white spectacles.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Black cap with white spectacles on male is unmistakable. Female's gray cap
also distinctive. Harsh, rapid song. Found in cedar-oak scrub in Texas.
