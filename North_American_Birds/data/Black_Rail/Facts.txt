Black Rail (Laterallus jamaicensis)
====================================

Description
-----------
Tiny, secretive rail barely larger than a sparrow. Males are dark slate gray
with chestnut nape and white spots on back. Red eye. Very small at 6 inches.
Females similar but browner. Extremely hard to see.

Behavior
--------
Moves through dense marsh vegetation like a mouse. Mostly nocturnal and
crepuscular. Distinctive kee-kee-kerr call heard at night. Rarely flies.

Habitat
-------
Dense saltmarshes and freshwater marshes along Atlantic and Gulf coasts, and
in California. Severely threatened by habitat loss and sea level rise. Rare.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Other rails are much larger. Sora has yellow bill. Virginia Rail has longer
red bill. Black Rail's tiny size and dark plumage are unique.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Tiny size and all-dark plumage in saltmarsh. Listen for kee-kee-kerr call at
night. Almost never seen well; usually only heard. Critically imperiled.
