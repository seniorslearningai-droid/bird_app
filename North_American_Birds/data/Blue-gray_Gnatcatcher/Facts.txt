Blue-gray Gnatcatcher (Polioptila caerulea)
============================================

Description
-----------
Tiny blue-gray bird with white eye ring, white underparts, long black tail
with white outer feathers; male has black eyebrow in breeding season.

Behavior
--------
Constantly active, flitting and flicking its long tail; gives a thin
squeaky "spee" call; builds a lichen-covered cup nest; forages in canopy.

Habitat
-------
Deciduous forests, woodlands, and thickets across much of North America
in summer; winters in southern U.S., Caribbean, and Central America.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Other gnatcatchers have different head patterns; Gray Vireo is larger and
lacks the tail-flicking; female Cerulean Warbler is larger and stockier.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Tiny blue-gray bird constantly flicking white-sided tail; thin squeaky
call; white eye ring; abundant in open woods across much of North America.
