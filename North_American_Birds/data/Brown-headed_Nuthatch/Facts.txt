Brown-headed Nuthatch (Sitta pusilla)
======================================

Description
-----------
Tiny nuthatch with brown cap, white cheeks and throat, blue-gray back and
wings, and buff underparts. Tiny white nape spot. About 4.5 inches. Uses
pine bark scales as tools — one of few U.S. tool-using birds.

Behavior
--------
Creeps headfirst down tree trunks. Uses bark flakes to pry up other bark.
Highly social, often in mixed flocks. High-pitched squeaky calls. Roosts
in cavities. Pairs cooperative breeders.

Habitat
-------
Mature longleaf and loblolly pine forests of the southeastern United States.
Requires open, park-like pine stands. Year-round resident; does not migrate.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Red-breasted Nuthatch has rust-orange underparts and black eyeline. White-
breasted Nuthatch is larger with white face. Pygmy Nuthatch of West similar.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Brown cap and tiny size in southeastern pine forests. High squeaky rubber-duck
call. Tool use with bark flakes. Pines of southeastern coastal plain.
