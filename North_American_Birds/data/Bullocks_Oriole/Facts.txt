Bullock's Oriole (Icterus bullockii)
=====================================

Description
-----------
Male has brilliant orange body with black back, wings, and crown. Large white
wing patch, black eyeline and throat. Female is yellowish-olive with whitish
belly. About 8.5 inches. Long tail.

Behavior
--------
Forages in tree canopy for insects, nectar, and fruit. Builds long, pendulous
woven nest hanging from branch tips. Males sing a rich, whistling song.

Habitat
-------
Open woodlands, riparian corridors, orchards, and parks in western North
America. Winters in Mexico and Central America. Common backyard visitor.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Baltimore Oriole (eastern) male has all-orange head. Hooded Oriole is thinner
with more orange and less black. Scott's Oriole has black hood in male.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Large white wing patch with black eyeline on orange male. Western range.
Female's yellowish tones with whitish belly. Hanging nest. Loud whistles.
