Clapper Rail (Rallus crepitans)
================================

Description
-----------
Large, secretive rail with gray-brown streaked upperparts, rusty-orange breast,
and gray cheeks. Long, slightly decurved bill. Barred flanks. About 14.5 inches.
Laterally compressed body for moving through marsh grass.

Behavior
--------
Wades and runs through dense saltmarsh grasses. Rarely flies. Gives loud,
cackling clapper call, especially at dawn and dusk. Difficult to observe.

Habitat
-------
Atlantic and Gulf coast saltmarshes from New England to Texas. Locally in
California marshes. Threatened by habitat loss and sea level rise.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
King Rail inhabits freshwater and has brighter rufous breast and barred flanks.
Virginia Rail is smaller with reddish bill. California Clapper Rail is a
subspecies.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Large rail in saltmarsh. Grating, accelerating cackle call is diagnostic.
Gray cheeks separate from King Rail. Saltwater marsh habitat. Rarely seen well.
