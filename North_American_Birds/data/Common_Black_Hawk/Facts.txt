Common Black-Hawk (Buteogallus anthracinus)
============================================

Description
-----------
Large, stocky hawk, nearly all black with single broad white tail band.
Short, broad wings. Yellow-orange cere and legs. About 21 inches with wide
wingspan. Immatures are brown with streaked underparts.

Behavior
--------
Wades into streams and pools to catch fish, crayfish, and frogs. Perches
low near water. Slow-flying. Nest in large riparian trees near permanent water.

Habitat
-------
Riparian corridors with large cottonwoods and sycamores along permanent streams
in Arizona, New Mexico, Utah, and Texas. Very limited U.S. range.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Zone-tailed Hawk mimics Turkey Vulture in flight but has banded tail.
Black-and-white Hawk-Eagle is tropical. Harris's Hawk has chestnut shoulders.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Large, all-black hawk near streams with single broad white tail band. Yellow
legs. Southwest riparian canyons. Wading behavior in water is distinctive.
