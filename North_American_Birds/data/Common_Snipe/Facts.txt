Common Snipe (Gallinago gallinago)
====================================

Description
-----------
Chunky shorebird with extremely long bill, striped brown and buff head,
heavily streaked brown back, and barred flanks. Short legs. About 10.5 inches.
Cryptic plumage, nearly invisible in marsh grass.

Behavior
--------
Probes soft mud and grass for earthworms and invertebrates with sensitive bill.
Produces "winnowing" sound with tail feathers during display flight. Flushed
birds burst upward in erratic zig-zag flight with rasping call.

Habitat
-------
Wet meadows, bogs, and marshes. Breeds across Canada and Alaska; winters in
U.S. south. Very similar to Wilson's Snipe; some taxonomists lump them.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Wilson's Snipe is nearly identical (North American species). American Woodcock
has different face pattern and rounder body. Long-billed Dowitcher much larger.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Extremely long bill and striped head in wet meadow habitat. Explosive flushing
with rasping call. Winnowing display flight overhead. Very similar to Wilson's Snipe.
