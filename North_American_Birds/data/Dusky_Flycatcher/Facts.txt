Dusky Flycatcher (Empidonax oberholseri)
=========================================

Description
-----------
Small, plain Empidonax flycatcher with olive-gray upperparts, whitish eye ring,
two white wing bars, and pale whitish-yellow underparts. About 5.75 inches.
Very difficult to distinguish from other Empidonax by sight alone.

Behavior
--------
Perches upright in open areas, sallying out for insects. Often pumps tail.
Song is the best identification feature: "chi-bit, p-wit, swee-it."

Habitat
-------
Brushy mountain slopes, chaparral, and open coniferous forest with shrubby
understory in western mountains. Winters in Mexico.

Similar Looking Birds
---------------------
Hammond's Flycatcher is nearly identical; best separated by voice and habitat.
Gray Flycatcher pumps tail downward more. Pacific-slope Flycatcher has yellow
underparts. All Empidonax require voice for confident identification.

Best Ways to Identify
---------------------
Voice is essential: song a slow, deliberate series of phrases. Brushy mountain
slopes in summer. Eye ring present. When singing, even beginners can identify.
